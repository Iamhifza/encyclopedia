## Simple Explanation

Human preference labelling is the expensive, slow, inconsistent part of alignment
— and for harmful content it means asking people to read a great deal of
unpleasant material.

RLAIF replaces the labeller with a model, guided by an explicit written set of
principles. The model critiques and revises its own output against those
principles, and the resulting comparisons train the reward signal.

The principles being *written down* is the part that matters most.

## Technical Definition

Preference training where comparison labels are generated by a model rather than
by humans. In Constitutional AI the procedure has two stages: a supervised stage
where the model critiques and revises its own responses against a constitution,
and a reinforcement stage where a preference model trained on AI-generated
comparisons supplies the reward.

## Why Does It Exist?

Human feedback does not scale. It is slow, costly, inconsistent between
annotators, and for harmful content it exposes people to material they should not
have to read repeatedly. It is also opaque — the values in an RLHF model are
implicit in thousands of individual judgements nobody wrote down.

## What Problem Does It Solve?

Cost and throughput of alignment data, and — the underrated benefit —
*legibility*. A constitution is a document you can read, criticise and amend. A
pile of annotator preferences is not.

## How Does It Work?

```text
STAGE 1 — supervised
  model responds ──▶ critique against a principle ──▶ revise ──▶ train on revision

STAGE 2 — reinforcement
  two responses ──▶ model judges which better follows the constitution
                        │
                preference data ──▶ reward model ──▶ policy optimisation
```

## Mental Model

A written code of conduct rather than a thousand individual judgements from a
thousand people. Both shape behaviour; only one can be inspected, debated and
revised.

## Example

The published comparison found AI feedback achieved comparable results to human
feedback on helpfulness and harmlessness, with far greater throughput. The
significant part is not the cost saving — it is that the values became a document.
You can disagree with a principle in a constitution. You cannot disagree with an
annotator's aggregate intuitions.

## Real-World Usage

Used in production alignment pipelines, frequently blended with human feedback
rather than replacing it entirely. The natural successor is RLVR, which removes
judgement altogether where a verifier exists — but verifiers only exist for
checkable tasks, so AI feedback remains necessary for everything subjective.

## Common Confusions

* **RLAIF vs RLVR** — a model judging against principles versus a program
  checking correctness. The first covers subjective qualities; the second is
  ungameable but narrow.
* **RLAIF vs distillation** — both use model-generated data. RLAIF generates
  *preferences* to train a reward signal; distillation copies behaviour.
* **The judge inherits its own biases** — using a model to supervise a model
  risks compounding shared blind spots, which is the standard objection and a
  reason human feedback has not disappeared.

## Why Should I Care?

It is how alignment training scaled beyond what human annotation could support,
and it introduced something genuinely valuable: model values expressed as a
document that can be read and argued with rather than inferred from behaviour.
