## Simple Explanation

Human-written training data is expensive and, at the top end, running short. So
models generate their own: instructions, solutions, dialogues, code — usually
filtered by a verifier or a stronger model before use.

## Technical Definition

Training examples generated by a model, typically with a filtering or
verification stage. Common forms: instruction generation and self-critique,
distillation from a stronger teacher, rejection sampling against a verifier
(keeping only solutions that pass tests), and targeted augmentation of
under-represented cases.

## Why Does It Exist?

High-quality human data for specialised domains is scarce and slow to produce,
while verification is often cheap. Where you can check an answer, you can
manufacture training data at scale.

## What Problem Does It Solve?

Data scarcity in narrow domains, coverage of rare cases, and cost of annotation.

## How Does It Work?

```text
seed prompts ──▶ generate many candidates ──▶ FILTER (verifier, tests,
                                              judge, dedupe, difficulty)
                                                       │
                                      keep the survivors ──▶ training set
```

The filter is the whole method. Unfiltered generation reproduces the generator's
existing distribution, including its errors.

## Mental Model

Practice problems written by a teacher, kept only if the answer key checks out.

## Example

The phi model family demonstrated that small models trained on carefully
generated "textbook-quality" data could outperform much larger models trained on
raw web text — evidence that data quality substitutes for scale within limits.

## Common Confusions

* **Synthetic data is not free capability** — without a verifier or a stronger
  teacher, a model cannot lift itself by its own output.
* **Model collapse risk** — training repeatedly on unfiltered machine-generated
  data degrades diversity and tail behaviour over generations.
* **Licensing** — generating training data from a commercial model's outputs
  usually violates its terms.

## Real-World Usage

Instruction tuning sets, reasoning traces filtered by verifiers, safety training
data for rare harmful cases, and evaluation sets for scenarios too rare to
collect.

## Why Should I Care?

Most post-training data in current models is synthetic in some form, and
understanding the filter is how you judge whether a training-data claim means
anything.
